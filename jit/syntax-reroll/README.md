# Syntax Reroll

> **A word moved. The world must now honor it.**

## Purpose

Syntax Reroll is an experiment in language as physical law. Literature compiles into persistent, navigable worlds. A reader can extract words from encountered prose and insert them elsewhere, forcing the existing world to reconcile the altered assertion without erasing its history.

This is **not** prompt-to-scene regeneration, a conventional text editor, or unlimited word conjuring. The core experiment is a bounded lexical intervention against a persistent reality.

## Canonical laws

1. **Text is reality.** Prose establishes worlds, inhabitants, objects, environments, and causal histories.
2. **Lexical conservation.** Words are extracted from encountered text, carried, and inserted elsewhere; they cannot be conjured freely. Preserve provenance.
3. **Extraction is not negation.** Removing *deserted* from a street withdraws that assertion; it does not automatically make the street crowded.
4. **Grammar survives.** Permit minimal grammatical or morphological repair, never unrelated semantic invention disguised as repair.
5. **Identity survives language.** Removing a name does not erase the person, place, or object it referred to.
6. **Causal history survives.** A lexical mutation does not automatically undo prior events or their consequences.
7. **Locality governs reconciliation.** Apply the smallest justified change that makes the altered language true. Do not regenerate the whole world.
8. **Future prose remains sealed.** Only encountered language is available for manipulation.
9. **Mutations are uncertain.** A change may have no observable effect, cause surprising consequences, or break a story; do not guarantee a dramatic result.
10. **Accepted consequences become history.** Accepted emergent results become canonical constraints on later reconciliations.

## Reference mutation

Source: “The **deserted** street stretched toward the sea.”

Extract **deserted**. The street is now described without that assertion; it is not necessarily crowded.

Destination: “The harbor lay beneath the moon.”

Insert the carried word: “The **deserted** harbor lay beneath the moon.”

Reconcile the harbor's existing reality with the new assertion. Its inhabitants, vessels, trade, or remembered events may constrain how desertion becomes true. Preserve what does not need to change.

## Interaction cycle

1. Inhabit and explore a persistent world.
2. Encounter language; establish available lexical inventory and provenance.
3. Extract a bounded token or phrase from encountered text.
4. Insert it at a grammatically valid destination, with minimal repair.
5. Perform **one bounded generative reconciliation** against current world state and history.
6. Inspect consequences; accept, reject, or intervene again.
7. Preserve accepted state and provenance for future interventions.

The model reconciles semantic pressure; it does not receive permission to replace the world.

## Invariants and design boundaries

- **Provenance:** Record source, destination, prior referent, and circumstances of extraction.
- **Persistence:** Track stable entities, their histories, and established causal relationships independently of the words that first named them.
- **Minimal change:** Separate withdrawn assertions from affirmative opposites; prefer local effects over global rewrites.
- **Sealed future:** No access to unseen prose as a token reservoir.
- **Bounded generation:** Reconciliation is a constrained transition, not a fresh creative-writing pass.
- **Exploration first:** Lexical intervention should be scarce and optional, not a mandatory action on every screen.
- **Possibility without arbitrariness:** Extraordinary outcomes are permitted when justified by the actual mutation and accumulated world history.
- **No false guarantees:** A prototype limit of two mutations per chapter was explored, but is not a universal law.
