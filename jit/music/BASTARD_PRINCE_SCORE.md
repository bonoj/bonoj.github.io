## User — original turn

The Tale of the Bastard Prince

I was born beneath the mountains where the molten rivers run
And I spent my youth in Arioch beneath the burning sun
My father was the King of Sand, my mother was a whore
But he loved my mother dearly and he loved me even more

He lavished us with wealth and land, and this displeased his wife
So he had his queen beheaded and her head placed on a spike
And then he named me for his heir and he took my mother's hand
The Bastard Prince of Arioch, and the Harlot Queen of Sand

Some noble houses grumbled, others hissed and raged and fumed
So my father summoned all their sons, and he had their heads removed
"My son will be your king," he said, "for all your sons are dead
Now kneel and swear him fealty, or I swear I'll have your heads"

And of course they knelt, of course they swore, they didn't want to die
But there was venom in their voices and there was hatred in their eyes
And before I reached my eleventh sun, they stormed the palace gates
And they beheaded both my parents, but I managed to escape

I stowed away aboard a ship and I crossed the Bitter Sea
And I spent three years in Amirinth in abject poverty
Until, one night, I met a girl who played a twelve stringed lute
And she took me to her father who was captain of a troupe

He saw that I was deft and quick, he saw that I was sharp
So he taught me how to sing and dance and to play the twelve string harp
I traveled with my roving troupe across the Shattered Plains
And I played in every little town and village on the way

And on the way, I learned some things, like how to fall to love
And how a girl who plays the lute can fit you like a glove
And how a girl who plays the lute can break your fragile heart
And smash your world to pieces as she breaks your twelve string harp

I left them then, my gypsy troupe, my vicious gypsy girl
And I wandered north to Eruvar, to the city made of pearl
And there I met another girl who played the violin
And she taught me how to string a bow and how to love again

And of course she also broke my heart, she broke my fiddle, too
But I learned my lesson from the first: You must always carry two
I met some other girls as well, not all of them could play
But each of them was special, so I gave my heart away

And when I tired of the ice and snow, I fiddled west and south
And I met a girl in Karrick with the most fantastic mouth
I mean this girl brilliant, you should have heard the things she said
I must admit, I learned a lot... both in and out of bed

And then I joined a band of thieves and I learned to move with stealth
And I picked a lot of pockets and amassed tremendous wealth
And after that, I headed south, I made for the Southern Tip
And I booked a royal cabin on a regal clipper ship

But the crew on board had other plans, they up and mutinied
"Join or hang," they told me, so I turned to piracy
I learned to reef and hand and steer, I learned to drink and swear
I learned to fight with swords and knives, and bring the guns to bear
I sailed along the Umbral Coast, I sank a thousand ships
And I drank a lot... a lot of rum... and I ate of a lot of fish

For twenty years I have wandered over seas and foreign lands
But I never quite forgot my home, my kingdom in the sand
So now I sail for Arioch to claim my rightful throne
The Bastard Prince in exile is finally coming home

---
Arioch - a massive, sprawling city in the arid wastes of a vast desert, Middle Eastern analogue
Amirinth - a bustling port city, Western European analogue
Eruvar, The Jewel of the North - a prosperous capital nestled on the slopes of a jutting chain of massive mountains, Eastern European / Russian analogue
Karrick - an ancient temple city at the outskirts of a vast jungle, Indian analogue

## Assistant — original reply

I would score *The Tale of the Bastard Prince* as a **bard's autobiography that gradually reveals itself to be a homecoming anthem**.

Not a cinematic soundtrack underneath the poem. A song the Bastard Prince himself could perform in a tavern, aboard a ship, or before the gates of Arioch.

And I wouldn't make it uniformly mournful. There's too much mischief, lust, violence, and absurdity in it. The music needs to know when to grin.

## The musical identity

**A wandering bard's ballad**

6/8 meter · D minor · ~92 BPM · solo baritone, twelve-string harp, fiddle, frame drum, low strings

The 6/8 meter gives it a rolling, almost horseback rhythm. It can become a sailor's jig without changing its underlying pulse, and it can slow into a lament when the story demands it.

I'd give it one recurring melodic idea: **the Prince's theme**. Four notes, initially played on a solitary plucked string, returning in different disguises throughout his travels.

**The Prince's motif · D minor**

D4 — F4 — A4 — F4

Illustrative motif: D–F–A–F. A minor triad rising toward something grand, then falling back toward its origin.

## The journey, scored

### I. The Blood of Arioch

![I. The Blood of Arioch](https://i.pinimg.com/originals/ab/a1/cb/aba1cb405f7d052bda7d58a9a51d0eb9.jpg?nii=t)

*Verses 1–4 · D minor · Solemn, then brutal*

A deep drone, twelve-string harp, and a lone voice. The king's love sounds warm, almost tender. When the heads begin falling, a low drum enters. At the palace massacre, the accompaniment disappears entirely.

### II. The Wandering Minstrel

![II. The Wandering Minstrel](https://char-gen.com/blog-images/dnd-ambience-generator-tavern-music.webp)

*Verses 5–7 · F major · Playful and youthful*

The twelve-string harp takes over. A lively, skipping rhythm; little instrumental answers between lines. The first heartbreak turns the major melody into minor without changing its notes much.

### III. The Jewel of the North

![III. The Jewel of the North](https://static.futureshareai.com/glb_features/ai_city_image_5.webp)

*Verses 8–9 · G minor · Beautiful, wry*

The fiddle arrives, singing a sweeping counter-melody. It's genuinely romantic until the broken fiddle line. Then a deliberate musical stumble, followed by a second fiddle answering the first. The audience should laugh.

### IV. Karrick and the Thieves

![IV. Karrick and the Thieves](https://media.aiclips.net/aiclips/aiclips/files/items/vobdbdxbl2eo/EKCSmU4s1PTKKgB_1742637592.webp)

*Verses 10–11 · D Dorian · Mischievous*

Plucked strings, hand percussion, and a dancing bass line. Short ornamental flourishes inspired by the city's musical setting. The singer delivers the joke about her mouth with a perfectly straight face; the instruments pause just long enough to let it land.

### V. The Umbral Coast

![V. The Umbral Coast](https://pooltattooz.com/cdn/shop/files/pirate-shiprev.png?v=1725101543)

*Verses 12–13 · A minor · Raucous*

The 6/8 becomes a rolling sea shanty. Frame drum, stomps, fiddle, and a rowdy chorus. The mutiny is punctuated by a sudden stop. By the thousand ships, the music has become gloriously excessive.

### VI. The Bastard Prince Returns

![VI. The Bastard Prince Returns](https://img3.wikia.nocookie.net/__cb20081108034229/pathfinder/images/b/b5/Absalom_skyline.jpg)

*Final verse · D minor → D major · Majestic, unresolved*

Everything falls away except the original harp and voice. The Prince's motif returns, slowly joined by the instruments acquired across his travels. On *“finally coming home”*, the whole ensemble rises into D major.

But the final chord contains a suspended note. He's coming home. We don't yet know whether he'll survive it.

## The crucial compositional choice

I would **not** write six unrelated pieces of music. I'd write one ballad with a consistent vocal melody, letting the accompaniment evolve as the Prince acquires experiences and instruments.

The harp he learned in Amirinth never entirely disappears. The fiddle from Eruvar joins it. The percussion of Karrick remains beneath the pirate rhythm. By the final verse, his entire life is audible.

And this is where our Ruined Orchestra becomes useful: instead of merely changing synthesizer presets, we can introduce *performers with musical memories*. Each instrument enters the story at a particular moment and returns later with its own variation of the Prince's theme.

One question matters before composing the actual vocal melody: **Do you hear this sung by one intimate, weathered male voice telling his own story, or by a full tavern of people who already know the legend?**

My instinct is the solitary voice, with the tavern gradually joining him—until the last verse, when everyone falls silent to hear what happens next.
