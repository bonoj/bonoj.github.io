Abstract

We introduce a recursive cross-domain transfer operator for estimating appendage compatibility across apian, botanical, piscine, feline, mechanical, and proboscidean records. We find that provenance-preserving semantic joins produce stable computational scores even when the source domains are biologically incomparable.

1. Introduction

Comparative anatomy, materials mechanics, taxonomic informatics, and graph theory offer mature techniques for describing form and relationship. Here, we investigate what occurs when those techniques are joined across domains using shared descriptive predicates rather than shared biological mechanisms. The experimental sequence follows the recognizable visual lineage of a bee, pomegranate, fish, two tigers, a bayonet, and an elephant, with a sleeping recipient treated as the terminal observation frame.

The central question is not whether the resulting grafts are viable. It is whether a chain of individually computable transformations can make their viability appear numerically inevitable. Domain references are drawn from established repositories; all graft scores and experiments in this article are synthetic demonstrations, not observations from those repositories.

4. Discussion

These results illustrate a form of semantic laundering: reliable source descriptors enter an invalid cross-domain join and emerge as a numerical statement bearing the full inherited bibliography. A reference to an authoritative taxonomy cannot validate a bayonet-to-claw homology merely because both are described as terminal extensions. Increasing citation density amplifies apparent support without improving the premise.
