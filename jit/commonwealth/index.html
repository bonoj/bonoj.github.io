The founding proposition

Human dignity is not an economic reward.

The world has extraordinary productive capacity. Yet too many lives remain defined by insecurity, dependency, and the inability to say no. Commonwealth begins with a different premise: every person deserves the material conditions to choose a life of their own.

The question of freedom

What does it mean to be free?

Freedom is more than the absence of restraint. It is the ability to refuse exploitation without losing your home, your healthcare, your food, or your future.

Security should belong to children from birth. It should survive illness, unemployment, disagreement, and misfortune.

The six guarantees
Our foundation / 01

Six guarantees.
Every human life.

These are proposed universal entitlements, not benefits conditioned on employment, citizenship, productivity, or allegiance to an institution.

01 / SHELTER

A place to belong.

Safe, stable housing and protection against arbitrary displacement.

02 / HEALTH

Care without ruin.

Medical, dental, preventive, mental and disability care.

03 / SUSTENANCE

Enough to live.

Food, clean water, sanitation and reliable essential energy.

04 / AGENCY

The power to refuse.

Economic security sufficient to resist coercive dependence.

05 / OPPORTUNITY

Room to become.

Education, connection, technology and meaningful mobility.

06 / LIBERTY

Rights that endure.

Privacy, due process, participation and protection from arbitrary power.

Our method / 02

From possibility to provision.

01 / UNDERSTAND

Map what exists.

Measure essential needs, productive capacity, infrastructure and uncertainty using verifiable evidence.

02 / DESIGN

Model what works.

Compare universal services, direct security and distributed ownership. Expose the trade-offs.

03 / GOVERN

Distribute power.

Combine public participation, independently selected human stewards and machine-assisted oversight.

04 / DELIVER

Prove the outcome.

Seek lawful, consenting institutional pilots. Audit delivery, preserve appeals and measure actual human security.

A constitutional commitment

Neither human nor machine should possess unanswerable power.

Every consequential decision must be explainable, contestable and reversible.

Stewardship / 03

Intelligence in service of humanity.

Machine mediation can make institutions more legible and decisions more accountable. It cannot appoint itself sovereign. Authority comes from people, legitimate institutions, and revocable mandates.

01 / HUMAN STEWARDS

Chosen, not anointed.

Open nominations, independent verification, public elections, civic lotteries, limited terms and accessible removal.

02 / MACHINE STEWARDS

Capable of disagreement.

Independent models investigate, simulate and challenge each other. No model consensus creates legal authority.

03 / INDEPENDENT AUDIT

Power that can be checked.

Separate evidence, authorization, custody, delivery and appeal. Preserve services while disputes are resolved.

The machinery of accountability

Build systems that can be challenged.

Commonwealth develops public models, constitutional tests and a synthetic council in which different allocation strategies can disagree without silently deciding for people.

The work is open for inspection. Research is not enforcement; a prototype is not a public mandate.
