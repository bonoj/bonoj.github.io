## The idea in one sentence

**Everstead™** is an elegant residential facility where eighteen fully grown **Continuants** are cultivated, conditioned, and allocated as replacement executives for organizations that prefer leadership continuity to human unpredictability.

**Public sign:** **EVERSTEAD**  
**Consumer-facing descriptor:** *Residential Care & Continuing Excellence*  
**Corporate disclosure:** *Everstead Center for Executive Replication, Maturation & Placement*  
**Tagline:** **Every future deserves familiar leadership.**  
**Secondary line:** *Because continuity is a form of care.*

No explicit “clone,” “replicant,” “batch” or “specimen” in hero copy. The word **Continuant** is the primary branded human designation. Internally and in subtle metadata, the 18 constitute the **current batch** — emphatically **not a cohort**. Avoid “cohort” anywhere in public UI. “Current batch” is the precise term in a tucked-away registry label, data attribute, or understated FAQ.

## Creative target and tone

A credible, established American continuing-care retirement community crossed with an executive retreat, a family foundation, and a corporate succession consultancy. The architecture is residential; language therapeutic; economics corporate; process industrial. The residents are not staff and not ordinary nursing-home patients. The portraits remain normal professional headshots; their names and appearance are identical to the 18 Golden Standard personnel. They are **current-batch Continuants** awaiting executive allocation or placement. The horror is the corporation's immaculate serenity and casual assumptions of human ownership, not gore, jump scares, overt villainy, or glitched interfaces.

**Primary rule:** The institution never admits it is sinister. Every line sounds like approved communications copy. The joke must remain legible on a second reading. Don't borrow a real facility's distinctive branding, falsely imply a real license, or incorporate any private patient facts from `bonoj/Home/concordia/`.
