# AI PSA — terminology panic

**Status:** idea captured; not approved for implementation.
**Home:** JIT Subversion.
**Premise:** A straight-faced anti-AI public-service campaign presents ordinary, benign software engineering terminology as evidence of alarming conduct. The claims can be literally true in their technical context, while the insinuations become absurd once that context is removed.

## Founding specimens

- **Dependency injection** — commonplace software design practice, reframed as AI systems “injecting dependencies.”
- **Race conditions** — ordinary concurrency defect, reframed as AI “exploiting race conditions.”

These are the anchors, not a mandate to inflate the list.

## Selection discipline

- Favor established terminology from general software engineering, not AI-specific research vocabulary.
- Favor unobtrusive, mundane technical terms that accidentally sound provocative, conspiratorial, or politically charged to non-engineers.
- Avoid obvious edgy engineer vocabulary (e.g. zombie processes, fork bombs, kill commands); the humor should arise from innocent language, not deliberate shock naming.
- The best allegations remain technically defensible when read literally, but their implied lay interpretation is wrong.
- Seek broadly recognizable anxieties rather than targeting actual groups with deceptive claims.
- Keep the institutional campaign sincere and polished; the audience discovers the misunderstanding.
- Do not pad the collection with weak examples.

## Possible form

A dead-serious anti-AI PSA campaign or public-awareness institution issuing ominous bulletins about familiar software practices. Its own site may quietly depend on the practices it condemns.

**Next step:** Only if the idea develops more strong specimens, reconsider a standalone build. No implementation requested yet.
