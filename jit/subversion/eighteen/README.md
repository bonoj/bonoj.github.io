## Premise
EIGHTEEN™ is a fictional, polished temporal staffing and casualty-assurance company. It offers one fixed package: the same eighteen reusable personnel, deployed anywhere in history where an engagement calls for eighteen recorded deaths. They return after each assignment, with different names, eras, and occupations but the same faces. The company sells continuity, certainty, and historical permanence, not an investigation into the phenomenon.

The website is EIGHTEEN™'s own customer-facing corporate service website. It treats historical events as a portfolio of completed engagements, and the eighteen people as a proud, extraordinarily tenured workforce. Its voice is confident, bright, helpful, meticulous, and oblivious to the moral absurdity. No fourth-wall-breaking fiction/satire disclaimers in in-world copy. Keep provenance, factual boundaries, and editorial notes here in the internal spec; assess any actual publishing obligations separately.

## Core product logic
- Exactly eighteen personnel, always the same cohort. No partial or expanded package.
- Placements may occur at any point in the timeline; dates need not be sequential.
- A completed engagement produces eighteen recorded fatalities; the personnel subsequently recover and redeploy.
- The client commissions an outcome in the fictional universe; actual historical causation and client identities are never asserted as fact.
- Death is a repeatable administrative event, with return-to-work reviews, benefits, performance management, and turnaround times.
- The service is absurdist speculative fiction, not a functional means of organizing violence or real-world harm.

## Information architecture (proposed, not yet approved)
1. **Home / Offering:** Hero, unambiguous eighteen-person guarantee, proof points, CTA.
2. **How It Works:** Consultation → temporal placement → archival confirmation → cohort recovery/redeployment. Explain fictional commercial process at a narrative level, never practical harmful mechanics.
3. **Selected Engagements:** Historical incident-inspired case studies, testimonials, archive filters (era, region, sector), dates and documented death counts.
4. **Our People:** Eighteen portraits, names, specialties, tenure, assignment histories, performance notes; consistent identities across projects.
5. **Request an Engagement:** Fictional theatrical intake, producing a mock engagement brief. Avoid actionable prompts for real targets, violence methods, or casualty procurement; use abstract narrative fields, fabricated scenarios, or safe presets.
6. **Client Relations / FAQ:** Fixed cohort policy, timeline availability, paperwork, recovery, repeat business, awkward exceptions.
7. **The Nineteenth Problem** (optional narrative seam): Anomalous record of nineteen deaths or a missing return; never over-explain on the marketing site.
