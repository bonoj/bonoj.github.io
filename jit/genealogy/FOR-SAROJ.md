## Meet Sarala Devi (1872–1945)

Sarala Devi Chaudhurani was born Sarala Ghosal in Calcutta, into the extraordinary nineteenth-century intellectual and political world of the Tagore family. Her mother, **Swarnakumari Devi**, was a writer and daughter of **Debendranath Tagore**. Swarnakumari's brother was **Rabindranath Tagore**: Sarala's maternal uncle, not her father. Her father was **Janakinath Ghosal**, associated with early Indian nationalist politics.

Sarala was a writer, editor, musician, organizer and advocate for women's education. She edited **Bharati**, promoted Indian-made goods through **Lakshmi/Lakkhir Bhandar**, encouraged physical culture and helped found the **Bharat Stree Mahamandal** in 1910. After marrying **Rambhuj/Rambhaj Dutt Chaudhary** in 1905, her political and family world also extended to **Lahore** and Punjab. She was not simply 'Tagore's niece'; she was a consequential figure in her own right.

Sources: [Government of India — Sarla Devi](https://amritmahotsav.nic.in/unsung-heroes-detail.htm?12540=), [Indian Express — her life and Jorasanko](https://indianexpress.com/article/lifestyle/sarala-devi-tagore-family-swadeshi-movement-bengali-revolutionary-6302759/), [Stree Shakti biographical sketch](https://www.streeshakti.com/bookS.aspx?author=7).

## Six rabbit holes worth opening

1. **The women of Jorasanko:** What was life like for Sarala, Swarnakumari and their relatives inside the Tagore household? Who wrote, studied, composed music and challenged convention?
2. **Lahore before Partition:** Follow Sarala's marriage, Punjabi political networks, women's education, newspapers and the later displacement of families.
3. **The Gandhi connection:** Sarala's son **Dipak** married **Radha**, daughter of **Maganlal Gandhi**. Who were they, and what happened to their descendants? Verify each generation rather than trusting a copied online tree.
4. **The mysterious Jagdish:** Search original family accounts for Jagdish, Jagdish Mohan, the other sons of Rambhuj, and possible name changes. Keep people with similar names separate until documentary evidence joins them.
5. **Your father's life:** Reconstruct Colonel Lalit Mohan Sinha's Army service, stations, retirement and death through official records, old family papers, school records and contemporary notices. A possible 'Col Lalit Mohan' entry in an undated 33 Assam Rifles directory is **unconfirmed**, especially given the approximate death window.
6. **Your own map of India:** Use remembered cities, languages, schools, homes, relatives, festivals, recipes, photographs and letters as clues to reconstruct a lived family geography. Memories can be valuable even when dates are fuzzy.

## How to treat the evidence

**Three labels are enough:**
- **Documented:** an original record or reliable historical account supports the claim.
- **Remembered:** a family member recalls it; preserve the exact words and uncertainty.
- **Hypothesis:** an interesting possibility still needing a connecting document.

Keep a note of **who said what, when, and where the source came from**. Do not quietly turn a guess into a parent-child link. Different spellings—Sarla/Sarala, Chaudhurani/Choudhury, Ghosal/Ghoshal, Rambhuj/Rambhaj—may refer to the same person, but identical common names can also refer to unrelated people.
