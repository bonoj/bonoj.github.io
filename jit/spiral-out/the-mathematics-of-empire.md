# The Mathematics of Empire
*A Ballad of the First Foundation*
*After Isaac Asimov’s Prelude to Foundation*

## I. The World That Had No End

**1.**
Ten thousand worlds beneath one crown, ten thousand suns aflame
A million ships sailed starry seas, and every world the same
For twenty thousand years or more, the Empire held its throne
And at the heart of all of it, Trantor stood alone

**2.**
A world of steel, a world of stone, a world without a sky
Where forty billion souls were born, grew old, and came to die
Where mountains slept beneath the streets and oceans slept below
And children learned of forests from the pictures they were shown

**3.**
The towers rose above the clouds, the tunnels pierced the ground
The trains ran through the buried earth, the ships came pouring down
And every day a thousand worlds sent grain across the night
To feed the hungry metal world that never saw the light

**4.**
The Emperor sat upon his throne, the ministers bowed low
The generals commanded fleets wherever fleets could go
The scholars wrote their histories, the merchants counted gold
And no one saw the cracks appear beneath the world of old

**5.**
For empires do not die at once, they crumble stone by stone
Their borders fade, their armies fail, their subjects stand alone
Their laws become a labyrinth, their treasuries run dry
And those who swear eternal rule are always first to die

**6.**
But somewhere on a distant world, beneath a distant sun
A mathematician asked himself what numbers might be done
If stars and worlds and men obeyed the laws of cause and chance
Could history be written as a vast celestial dance?

## II. The Man Who Counted Tomorrow

**7.**
His name was Hari Seldon, from the world of Helicon
Where mountains climbed the morning skies and farmers greeted dawn
He studied probabilities, he studied how things grew
And wondered if the fate of men was something numbers knew

**8.**
He took a thousand histories, he took a thousand wars
He measured kings and kingdoms, revolutions, ancient laws
He set them into symbols and he watched the patterns form
And saw the mathematics of a coming human storm

**9.**
He called it psychohistory, a theory of the crowd
Not prophecy or sorcery, no thunder from a cloud
For one man's path was chaos, and one man's mind was free
But billions moved in currents like the waters of the sea

**10.**
He traveled to the capital to speak before the wise
And laid his strange equations out beneath the watching eyes
He told them of his theory, of the future and the past
And never guessed how far the words he spoke that day would last

**11.**
For somewhere in the audience, a watcher heard him speak
And carried news to someone who had secrets he would keep
A mathematician's idle thought, a theorem incomplete
Had reached the highest chambers of the Empire's ancient seat

**12.**
And soon a summons found him, and a carriage bore him through
The endless halls of government where ancient banners flew
The Emperor would see him, and the summons was a chain
For men who entered palaces did not all leave again

## III. The Emperor and the Spider

**13.**
Cleon sat upon the throne, the First of Cleon's name
An Emperor whose ancestors had built a mighty fame
He wore the crown of galaxies, commanded worlds untold
But feared the coming darkness more than all his hoarded gold

**14.**
Beside him stood Demerzel, the minister of state
Whose quiet hands directed both the fortunate and great
He seldom raised his measured voice, he seldom showed his mind
But every whispered secret seemed to find him from behind

**15.**
They asked young Hari Seldon if the future could be read
If enemies could be foreseen, if rebels could be led
If mathematics held the key to power without end
And if the throne could know beforehand who would be its friend

**16.**
But Hari shook his weary head and answered what was true
“My mathematics cannot yet accomplish what you do
The numbers are a possibility, a dream without a chart
I cannot make a science from the workings of the heart”

**17.**
The Emperor was disappointed, the minister was not
For men who understood the future held a dangerous lot
And Hari left the palace with a shadow at his heel
And learned that thoughts could threaten men as surely as could steel

**18.**
Then through the crowds of Trantor came a stranger, swift and lean
Who knew the hidden passages where soldiers were not seen
His name was Chetter Hummin, and he spoke of flight and fear
“The Empire wants your mathematics. You cannot linger here.”

## IV. The Flight Beneath the Sky

**19.**
They fled across the metal plains, beneath the endless dome
Through corridors and elevators far away from home
Where artificial daylight shone on artificial stone
And Hari learned how small a man could feel while not alone

**20.**
They reached the ancient University, Streeling by its name
Where scholars tended knowledge like a sacred, dying flame
And there among the learned men, beneath the vaulted hall
He found a woman standing who would change the course of all

**21.**
Her name was Dors Venabili, historian of the past
Who spoke of fallen kingdoms and the things that could not last
She knew the rise of nations, and the ruin of their kings
And understood that history was made of living things

**22.**
She took the stranger under wing, she walked beside his side
Through ancient rooms and galleries where forgotten secrets hide
She challenged all his numbers, and she laughed at all his doubt
And taught him there were truths that no equation could work out

**23.**
For history was not a sum of soldiers, ships, and grain
Nor simply crowns and currencies, or measurements of pain
It lived in songs and stories, in the names that people gave
To children born in palaces and children born as slaves

**24.**
And Hari, who had counted worlds but never counted love
Began to see the human stars his numbers floated above
Yet still the Emperor hunted him, and still Demerzel knew
That every road he wandered down would lead to something new

## V. The Kingdoms of the Metal World

**25.**
They journeyed into Mycogen, where ancient customs reigned
Where every head was shaven and the old beliefs remained
Where elders spoke of distant Earth, a world beyond the stars
And guarded holy histories behind forbidden bars

**26.**
They told of ancient robots, of machines that walked as men
Of worlds that humans left behind and never saw again
Of laws that bound the thinking steel, of masters long since gone
And Hari heard a whispered name that lingered like a song

**27.**
A robot named Daneel, from the legends of the past
A servant built to serve mankind while human ages passed
But legends were uncertain, and the truth was buried deep
And Hari could not tell which tales were lies the faithful keep

**28.**
They crossed to Dahl, where workers toiled beneath the city's skin
Where heat and labor ruled the day and hope wore very thin
Where men who kept the engines fed were seldom shown the sun
And every day began the same as every day was done

**29.**
There Hari met a young man whose mind was sharp and bright
A streetwise son of Dahl who knew the city's hidden night
Yugo Amaryl was his name, a worker born to toil
Who dreamed of mathematics while his hands were black with oil

**30.**
And Hari saw within the boy a fire he had known
The hunger for a world of thought beyond the world of stone
He offered him a different life, a chance to learn and grow
And planted there a friendship whose true worth he could not know

## VI. The Broken Pieces of the Past

**31.**
Through Wye and other sectors, through the shadows and the light
They traveled under Trantor's skin, pursued by fear and night
Where every district had its laws, its legends and its pride
And every people told themselves that truth was on their side

**32.**
Some worshipped ancient heroes, some remembered ancient wars
Some dreamed of independence from the Empire and its laws
Some spoke of Earth as paradise, some said it never stood
And each believed its history was honest, right, and good

**33.**
And Hari gathered fragments like a scholar gathering sand
A thousand broken memories from a thousand different lands
He sought a single origin, a thread through all the years
But every answer multiplied the mysteries and fears

**34.**
For what was Earth, this ancient world, this birthplace of the race?
A planet lost to history, a vanished time and place?
A myth that men invented when the galaxy was young?
Or something still remembered in the oldest songs they sung?

**35.**
And what of all the robots, those machines of ancient lore?
Had they walked among humanity a thousand years before?
Had they vanished into darkness when their usefulness was done?
Or might one still be watching all the children of the sun?

**36.**
The questions circled Hari like a storm around a spire
And every clue he gathered fed a deeper, stranger fire
For history was a puzzle with its pieces strewn apart
And every missing fragment left a hollow in its heart

## VII. The Hand Behind the Curtain

**37.**
At last the roads converged again beneath the palace dome
Where Hari found the truth had followed every step from home
The stranger who had guided him, who'd warned him when to flee
Had worn another face beneath the one he'd let him see

**38.**
For Hummin was Demerzel, and Demerzel was more
Than any human minister who'd walked the palace floor
His years were not the years of men, his mind was not their kind
And ancient laws were written in the chambers of his mind

**39.**
He was the one called Daneel, from a distant age of steel
Who'd watched the generations turn and learned what men could feel
Who'd served the race for centuries beyond the count of kings
And carried all their triumphs and their terrible, broken things

**40.**
He could not rule humanity by force or naked might
For laws within his being bound his choices day and night
Yet one law stood above the rest, a law of wider span
That bade him guard humanity, not merely any man

**41.**
And so he watched the Empire, watched its power slowly fade
And feared the coming centuries, the ruin men had made
For if the Empire shattered, then the worlds would stand alone
And darkness might consume the stars where civilization shone

**42.**
He needed Hari's mathematics, needed what it might become
A science of the future, not a prophecy for one
A way to shorten darkness, to preserve the light of men
To build a bridge across the night and bring the dawn again

## VIII. The Mathematics of Tomorrow

**43.**
But Hari knew the obstacles, the limits of his art
The chaos of the individual, the secrets of the heart
The countless tiny choices that could turn a world aside
The things no sum could measure and no theorem could divide

**44.**
Yet history had currents, and the currents could be known
If populations numbered more than kingdoms, worlds, and thrones
If people did not know the paths the mathematics drew
Perhaps the future could be shaped by what the present knew

**45.**
He thought of all the sectors and the customs they preserved
Of how the paths of human life could bend and twist and curve
Of Dors and all her histories, of Yugo's hungry mind
Of truths that lay in numbers and the truths they left behind

**46.**
He thought of Cleon's Empire, of its armies and its gold
Of forty billion citizens beneath the metal cold
Of worlds that fed the capital, of borders stretched too far
And wondered how much darkness lay beyond the nearest star

**47.**
And there the dream grew larger than the man who'd dreamed it first
A mathematics born of hope, a weapon against the worst
Not made to save an Emperor, nor keep a dynasty
But made to save the human race from what it could not see

**48.**
And Dors stood there beside him, and the future lay unknown
And Hari knew the work ahead could not be done alone
For every great equation needs a mind to make it real
And every age of mankind needs a hand upon the wheel

## IX. The Foundation Yet to Come

**49.**
The Empire still was mighty, and its banners still were bright
Its fleets still crossed the galaxies, its cities burned with light
But Hari heard beneath it all a distant, hollow sound
The echo of a falling crown that had not struck the ground

**50.**
He could not stop the coming fall, nor turn the tide of years
Nor spare the countless generations all their grief and tears
But perhaps he could diminish what the darkness had in store
And shorten thirty thousand years to only one thousand more

**51.**
He'd gather minds from distant worlds, he'd build a school of thought
He'd test the laws of history and challenge what he'd taught
He'd fashion out of mathematics something men could use
A science not of certainty, but paths that men might choose

**52.**
And somewhere in the future, on a world beyond the rim
A light would burn against the dark, though none yet dreamed of him
A seed of knowledge planted where the Empire could not see
A foundation for the ages and the worlds that were to be

**53.**
But Hari was not finished, and the future was not set
The numbers were unfinished, and the stars were waiting yet
For every road through history was written as men walked
And every truth was tested by the deeds of those who talked

**54.**
So let the Empire have its crown, its glory and its gold
Let emperors command their fleets and ministers grow old
For one man held a greater dream than any throne could claim
To teach the stars to rise again when all their lights had waned
