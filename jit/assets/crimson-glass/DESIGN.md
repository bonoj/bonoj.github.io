# Consequence-First Miniature Simulation
## Design thesis — Crimson / Glass

**Animate consequences at miniature scale, not actors.**

A small pressure module inside a much larger orbital station receives a violent impact. Red spray strikes the glass, adheres, drips, and pools. The compartment goes dark. The pressure indicator falls. A bulkhead closes. Without rendering a single person, the observer understands that everyone inside may have died.

This is environmental storytelling generated by causal simulation rather than scripted character animation.

## Core principle

**The environment is the witness.** Inhabitants need not be explicitly rendered to be narratively present. Their activity, absence, and fate are legible through persistent changes to their surroundings. The world should not merely play an animation on an event; an event should change the world, and those changes should generate observable consequences.

Avoid falsely equating any single visual cue with a proven casualty count. Red spray can imply injury or death in the fiction; whether everyone died must follow from the simulated conditions, not the visual alone.

## A language of physical consequences

| Event | Visible consequence | Persistent state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Violence / internal impact | Red spray on glazing, cracked windows, displaced objects, flickering lights, silence | Adhered stains, damage, displaced matter |
| Decompression | Vapor rush, moisture crystallization, collapsing membranes, debris pulled toward breach | Pressure loss, frost, open rupture, redistributed debris |
| Thermal catastrophe | Blistering paint, deforming seals, glowing metal, propagating heat | Structural degradation, thermal fields, failed components |
| Abandonment | Condensation, plant overgrowth, settled dust, last fan stopping | Ecology, residue, atmospheric changes, equipment state |

Visuals are evidence of physical processes, not interchangeable event icons.

## Three observational scales — one underlying event

1. **Station:** a compartment goes dark; adjacent modules isolate; system-level indicators change.
2. **Module:** impact, breach, pressure collapse, emergency closure, environmental motion become legible.
3. **Glass:** individual droplets splatter, adhere, stretch into rivulets, drip, and pool below.

A single causal event is projected at different scales. Zooming should change *what can be resolved*, not substitute an unrelated animation or a new history.

## Simulation model

A station is a graph of compartments connected by apertures, seals, doors, pipes, windows, and shared structural members. Each compartment carries state; connections transmit matter, heat, pressure, and energy under appropriate constraints.

Minimum candidate state:
- Atmosphere: gas inventory, pressure, temperature, composition.
- Fluids: volume/mass, adhesion, viscosity, surface wetting, runoff, pooling.
- Structure: integrity, fractures, apertures, permeability, deformable seals.
- Energy: power supply, heat, lights, equipment state.
- Environment: frost, condensation, dust, debris, biological growth where applicable.
- Occupancy: optional abstract population and survival conditions; **no actor animation required**.

Conservation and transfer rules matter more than exhaustive molecular simulation. Use cheap aggregate models at station scale and selective higher-resolution simulation at the point of observation.

### Example causal chain

Micrometeorite impact → structural damage → opening to vacuum → gas flow and pressure loss → temperature/moisture effects → debris motion → bulkhead isolation → power and lighting changes → persistent residue and damage.

If the scenario includes internal violence, the crimson fluid is a separate material event with its own source and mass; it should not appear solely because pressure falls.

## Persistence: physical memory

Consequences must survive beyond their initial effect:
- A stain stays until washed, abraded, burned, or otherwise removed.
- Frost stays until environmental conditions melt or sublimate it.
- Debris remains where it comes to rest or is subsequently moved.
- A bulkhead remains closed until commanded or physically altered.
- Heat and damage propagate rather than resetting at the end of a clip.
- Plant growth, condensation, and dust reveal elapsed time and system health.

An observer arriving hours later should be able to reconstruct plausible history from the remaining physical evidence. The environment becomes an archaeological record of events.

## Rendering and computational strategy

**Do not animate thousands of characters.** Simulate state changes, transfers, and visible consequences.

- **Macro:** compartment graph, low-frequency systems state, event propagation.
- **Meso:** localized module geometry, seals, apertures, lights, loose objects.
- **Micro:** material-specific splashes, rivulets, droplets, frost fronts, deformation.
- **Level of detail:** allocate simulation and rendering effort near visible, causally relevant regions. Distant modules retain summarized state and event histories.
- **Reproducibility:** log causes, transitions, conserved quantities, and timestamps; reconstruct visual evidence from state when feasible.
- **Material vocabulary:** effects are driven by properties such as viscosity, adhesion, pressure differential, heat capacity, and permeability, not merely animation names.

This is a design direction, not a claim that the current toy implements the full model.

## Current specimen: Crimson / Glass

Source: [index.html](./index.html)  
Live: https://bonoj.github.io/jit/assets/crimson-glass/

The existing experiment demonstrates projected 3D glass and floor, red splatter, adhered droplets, trails, airborne particles, and pooling, with blast, orbit, viscosity, spread, pause, and reset controls.

**Known limitations:** Canvas2D projection rather than a true 3D volumetric solver; approximate adhesion and runoff; expensive per-droplet rendering and retained trail geometry; no compartment pressure system, conservation audit, collision-accurate glass, or event-level narrative engine. Its performance is intentionally deferred for later optimization. Do not misrepresent it as a complete simulation.

## Suggested first vertical slice

A single transparent pressure module connected to one neighboring compartment:
1. Establish stable pressurized state and observable environmental cues.
2. Apply one impact and open a physical breach.
3. Let pressure and temperature evolve, with a bulkhead reacting.
4. Add crimson material splatter only when the scenario contains a corresponding source.
5. Persist stains, frost, debris, damage, and door state.
6. Orbit and zoom from station overview to the glass without changing the event's causal history.
7. Instrument frame time, particle counts, state transitions, and material accounting before optimization.

### Success criteria

- The viewer can infer the major event without an actor or explanatory text.
- Zoom levels reveal consistent evidence of the same event.
- Effects arise from state changes and remain after the event.
- Nearby compartments react according to their connections.
- Runtime expense is measured rather than hidden.
- A later observer can inspect the aftermath and infer a plausible sequence.

## North star

**We do not need to render life to make a world feel inhabited. We need to render what life leaves behind—and what happens when it stops.**
